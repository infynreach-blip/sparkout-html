Frequently Asked Questions

The agile software development life cycle is a flexible, iterative process that includes planning, designing, developing, testing, deploying, and maintaining software via short sprints, thus ensuring rapid delivery and continuous improvements.

The best practices include sprint retrospectives, daily stand-ups, backlog grooming, cross-functional teams, continuous integration, and regular client reviews. All these are carried out to ensure high-quality software with aligned stakeholder goals.

The cost varies based on features, integrations, sprint duration, team size, etc. At Sparkout, we deliver MVPs that start at $10,000, while our enterprise-grade systems may exceed $80,000 based on complexity and scope.

It breaks the project into manageable parts, allowing features to be built, tested, and improved continuously. This reduces rework, accelerates delivery, and increases user satisfaction.

Agile follows iterative cycles that are incremental and allow continuous feedback and delivery, whereas waterfall is linear with defined phases and a final release. i.e., Agile adapts quickly, while waterfall is rigid.
